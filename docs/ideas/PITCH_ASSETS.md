# Geppetto pitch: verified visuals and facts

Checked 2026-11-14. Licence and pixel sizes were read from the Commons API and file pages. "PD" means Commons tags it public domain. "CC0" means the holding museum released its scan with no restrictions. Both need no credit, but a small credit line is polite.

## Recommendation up top

- Beat 2 (quote card): use the Uffizi Baptism, PD, 3760 x 4520. Crop the left angel from it.
- Beat 3 (master and apprentices): use Stradanus "Color Olivi", Rijksmuseum scan, CC0, 5568 x 4072. Your guess is right, with two caveats below.
- Warning: the two biggest Baptism files on Commons (5200 x 6210 and 2906 x 3438) are CC BY-SA 4.0, NOT public domain. Avoid them. Both are photos with their own licence.
- Script flag: Leonardo was born in 1452 and joined Verrocchio's shop around 1466 (aged about 14), so "teenager" is true of when he arrived, not of the 1470s. In the 1470s he was 18 to 27. Safer line: "One of his apprentices was a teenager called Leonardo" is fine if you do not pin it to the 1470s, or say "In Florence in the 1460s".

## A. Beat 2 hook: The Baptism of Christ

Facts (all checked):
- Title: The Baptism of Christ. Artists: Andrea del Verrocchio and Leonardo da Vinci. Date: about 1470-1475 per the museum. Tempera and oil on panel, 177 x 151 cm. Uffizi, Florence, inventory 1890 n. 8358. [Uffizi page](https://www.uffizi.it/en/artworks/verrocchio-leonardo-baptism-of-christ)
- Leonardo and the left angel: the Uffizi says Vasari tells that the young pupil painted the angel on the left. [Uffizi](https://www.uffizi.it/en/artworks/verrocchio-leonardo-baptism-of-christ)
- Modern research says Leonardo did more than the angel (landscape, golden light, Christ, in oil), and maybe finished it as late as 1476-8, when he was no longer a true apprentice. [National Gallery, Dunkerton 2010](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/media/15403/dunkerton_syson_2010b.pdf), [Dunkerton 2011](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/media/15697/dunkerton2011.pdf)
- Another older painter probably worked on it too (Francesco Botticini is suggested). [Dunkerton 2011](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/media/15697/dunkerton2011.pdf)

Vasari, De Vere translation (Life of Leonardo, Project Gutenberg #28420, vol. IV):
> "...his angel was much better than the figures of Andrea; which was the reason that Andrea would never again touch colour, in disdain that a child should know more than he."

Source: [Gutenberg 28420](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/28420/pg28420-images.html) (also in the Fordham PDF of the same translation: [Fordham](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/vasari/vasari-lives.pdf)). The Fordham sourcebook HTML page carries a different, older wording ("never touching colours again"), so quote from the Gutenberg or Fordham PDF text above.

FLAG AS LEGEND. Do not say "Verrocchio gave up painting" as fact.
- Vasari wrote a century later, and it was a late addition (1568 edition). [Dunkerton 2011](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/media/15697/dunkerton2011.pdf)
- A museum-press article calls it "a narrative device rather than an episode that actually occurred". [Finestre sull'Arte](https://www.finestresullarte.info/en/works-and-artists/verrocchio-and-leonardo-da-vinci-s-baptism-of-christ-when-pupil-and-master-collaborate)
- Safe spoken line: "Legend says the master put down his brush for good."

## B. Beat 3: one master, a room of apprentices (ranked by 2-second clarity)

1. **Stradanus (Jan van der Straet), Color Olivi (Invention of Oil Painting), Nova Reperta plate 14.** Verified. A master paints a large St George and the Dragon at the centre. Around him: a boy copying a bust, boys grinding and preparing colour, a portrait painter, two men inking plates, a boy arriving with a panel. Engraved by Jan Collaert I or Theodoor Galle, published by Philips Galle, Antwerp. Designed in Florence about 1588-9, printed about 1590-1600 (sources vary, so say "c. 1590"). Caption: "Colorem olivi commodum pictoribus, Invenit insignis magister Eyckius" (the famous Master Eyck found oil paint for painters). [RMG](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-101943), [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/659725), [BnF](https://essentiels.bnf.fr/fr/enseignants/084c639d-a915-453d-8ed8-a145c12cda6a-dans-atelier-un-artiste-la-renaissance)
   - Files (all pages exist):
     - [Rijksmuseum scan, RP-P-1904-1038](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Schilderen_met_olieverf_Color_olivi_(titel_op_object)_Nieuwe_uitvindingen_en_ontdekkingen_(serietitel)_Nova_Reperta_(serietitel),_RP-P-1904-1038.jpg): 5568 x 4072, CC0. Use this one.
     - [Met scan, plate 14](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:New_Inventions_of_Modern_Times_-Nova_Reperta-,_The_Invention_of_Oil_Painting,_plate_14_MET_DP841119.jpg): 3424 x 2594, CC0.
     - [British Museum scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Color_olivi_(BM_1948,0410.4.204).jpg): 1600 x 1209, PD.
   - Caveats: (a) it is late 16th century (not 1400s), made by a Flemish artist in Florence, and it shows the imagined workshop of the oil-paint inventor Jan van Eyck, not an Italian bottega. Do not caption it as van Eyck's real studio, caption it "A Renaissance painter's workshop, c. 1590". (b) It is a line engraving, black and white, small figures. A zoom into the centre helps.
   - The Rijksmuseum credits the date range "ca. 1593 - ca. 1598" on Commons, the Met says "ca. 1600", the Art Institute says "c. 1591". So print "c. 1590s".
2. **Agostino Veneziano after Baccio Bandinelli, The Academy of Baccio Bandinelli in Rome (1531).** A teaching scene: students draw a model by candlelight round a table while the master sits at the right with a figure in his hands. Rijksmuseum scan, [file page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Academie_van_beeldhouwer_Baccio_Bandinelli,_RP-P-OB-36.662.jpg), 3952 x 3640, CC0 (Rijksmuseum RP-P-OB-36.662, engraving, 1531). Very clear "master plus students", strong shadows, good for drama. Weakness: it is a drawing academy, not a bottega with grinding of paint. WGA lower-res alternative: [WGA16405](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Agostino_dei_Musi_-_The_Academy_of_Baccio_Bandinelli_in_Rome_-_WGA16405.jpg), 1096 x 1000, PD.
3. **Stradanus, Nova Reperta plate 19, The Invention of Copper Engraving.** An engraver's workshop with a master and assistants. Met scan, [file page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:New_Inventions_of_Modern_Times_-Nova_Reperta-,_The_Invention_of_Copper_Engraving,_plate_19_MET_DP841117.jpg), 3552 x 2655, CC0, c. 1600. Good second choice if you want a craft-hands feel (hands at work). I have not checked in detail who is where in the scene, so look before you use it.
4. **Other Nova Reperta plates** (distillation, silk, printing) are also on Commons as CC0 Met scans, e.g. [plate 7, Distillation](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:New_Inventions_of_Modern_Times_-Nova_Reperta-,_The_Invention_of_Distillation,_plate_7_MET_DP841129.jpg) (3544 x 2677). Workshops, but not art or apprentices as the main subject. Weaker for "master and students".

Not found: I did not find a clean, Commons-hosted, public-domain St Luke painting scene with visible assistants, so I am not recommending one. Treat as unverified rather than "does not exist".

Ranking reason: Color Olivi shows one central master plus at least six people of different ranks at work in one room, and a guide text (BnF) describes it as about ten people around a master. It reads in 2 seconds. Bandinelli reads even faster as "teacher and students", but is a drawing school.

## D. Bottega facts worth saying aloud

Sources disagree, so say "roughly" and prefer ranges.

- **Cennini's ideal training (Libro dell'Arte, c. 1400): about 13 years.** One year drawing on small panels, six years shop craft (grinding colours, boiling glues, gesso, gilding), six more learning to paint and fresco. The National Gallery of Art notes this was "considerably longer than usually occurred". [NGA teaching resource](https://www.italianrenaissanceresources.com/units/unit-3/essays/training-and-practice), Cennini ch. 104 in [translation PDF](https://www.paduan.dk/Kunsthistorie%202008/Tekster/Cennino%20Cennini%20-%20On%20Painting.pdf)
- **Real contracts were shorter:** Venice 2 years minimum before journeyman, Padua 3 years minimum. [NGA](https://www.italianrenaissanceresources.com/units/unit-3/essays/training-and-practice). A summary site says one to eight years recorded, commonly three to five, but it is a secondary source, so treat as UNVERIFIED: [methods.art](https://methods.art/academies/renaissance-bottega).
- **Start age:** some boys were under 10 (Andrea del Sarto at 7), most were 3 or 4 years older, so about 10 to 14. Michelangelo went in at 13 to Ghirlandaio, a three-year contract. [NGA](https://www.italianrenaissanceresources.com/units/unit-3/essays/training-and-practice), [methods.art](https://methods.art/academies/renaissance-bottega)
- **First jobs were chores:** apprentices (garzoni) prepared panels and ground pigments, then learned to draw by copying the master's drawings. [NGA](https://www.italianrenaissanceresources.com/units/unit-3/essays/training-and-practice)
- **Leonardo:** not apprenticed to Verrocchio until he was 14 or 15 (secondary source, from Peter Burke). [shafe.co.uk](http://www.shafe.co.uk/essay_panel_painters_workshop). Works with the Commons "work period from 1466" field. Documents place him still "with Andrea del Verrocchio" in 1476. [Dunkerton 2010](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/media/15403/dunkerton_syson_2010b.pdf)
- **Verrocchio's students:** Leonardo, Perugino and Botticelli are named in the World History Encyclopedia ([source](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1611/life-in-a-renaissance-artists-workshop)); the Botticelli claim is weaker, Khan Academy only names Leonardo and Perugino ([Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/renaissance-reformation/early-renaissance1/beginners-renaissance-florence/a/the-role-of-the-workshop-in-italian-renaissance-art)). Safe to say Leonardo and Perugino.
- **Workshop size: UNVERIFIED.** One course page says "as many as thirty apprentices and assistants" in some workshops, without a primary source. Do not use for "only so many people in a room". The Color Olivi print itself shows about ten people. [shafe.co.uk](http://www.shafe.co.uk/essay_panel_painters_workshop), [BnF](https://essentiels.bnf.fr/fr/enseignants/084c639d-a915-453d-8ed8-a145c12cda6a-dans-atelier-un-artiste-la-renaissance)
- **Uffizi on workshop practice:** "It was usual, in 15th-century artist's studios, for the studio head to design the piece, leaving the secondary parts to be painted by pupils and collaborators." That supports the "apprentice learns by doing real work for the master" framing. [Uffizi](https://www.uffizi.it/en/artworks/verrocchio-leonardo-baptism-of-christ)
