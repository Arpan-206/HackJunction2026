# Renaissance quotes for the daily-quote panel

All quotes were checked against the actual English text of the cited edition (Project Gutenberg text, archive.org scan or the noteaccess.com transcription of Spencer and Thompson). Wording is exact. "..." marks an omission. Italian is given only where I saw it in an Italian edition; otherwise it says "not checked".

Confidence: HIGH = wording and location both read in the cited edition. MEDIUM = wording verified, page number inferred.

Suggested short credit line in the app: "Cennino Cennini, c. 1400, trans. D. V. Thompson". Keep the author, work and chapter on each card.

## Quotes

### 1. Cennini on starting with a master (already verified)
- English: "And begin to submit yourself to the direction of a master for instruction as early as you can; and do not leave the master until you have to."
- Italian: "E quanto più tosto puoi, incomincia a metterti sotto la guida del maestro a imparare; e quanto più tardi puoi, dal maestro ti parti."
- Author and work: Cennino Cennini, Il Libro dell'Arte, chapter III ("Fundamental provisions for anyone who enters this profession").
- Edition: The Craftsman's Handbook, trans. Daniel V. Thompson Jr., Yale University Press, 1933 (Dover reprint 1954), pp. 2-3.
- Seen at: https://noteaccess.com/Texts/Cennini/1.htm and https://archive.org/details/illibrodellarte1933cenn
- Italian seen at: https://www.didatticarte.it/Blog/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/cennini_il_libro_dell_arte.pdf (Tambroni/Milanesi-line text, chapter III)
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Note: the sentence before it lists the four virtues "Enthusiasm, Reverence, Obedience, and Constancy" (Italian: amore, timore, ubbidienza e perseveranza). Good as a second card if wanted.

### 2. Cennini on drawing a little every day
- English: "Do not fail, as you go on, to draw something every day, for no matter how little it is it will be well worth while, and will do you a world of good."
- Italian: "Continuando ogni dì non manchi disegnar qualche cosa, chè non sarà sì poco che non sia assai; e faratti eccellente pro."
- Author and work: Cennino Cennini, Il Libro dell'Arte, chapter XXVIII ("How, beyond masters, you should constantly copy from nature with steady practice").
- Edition: Thompson, Yale University Press, 1933, p. 15.
- Seen at: https://noteaccess.com/Texts/Cennini/1a.htm ; Italian at the didatticarte PDF above (chapter XXVIII, Italian edition p. 81).
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Tip: 30 words in full. For a shorter card cut after "worth while" and mark the cut.

### 3. Cennini on a hand trained by good models
- English: "...your hand and your mind, being always accustomed to gather flowers, would ill know how to pluck thorns."
- Italian: not checked.
- Author and work: Cennino Cennini, Il Libro dell'Arte, chapter XXVII ("How you should endeavor to copy and draw after as few masters as possible").
- Edition: Thompson, Yale University Press, 1933, p. 15 (the page marker falls just before chapter XXVIII, so p. 14-15).
- Seen at: https://noteaccess.com/Texts/Cennini/1a.htm
- Confidence: HIGH on wording, MEDIUM on exact page.
- Note: the ellipsis opens mid-sentence. In full the sentence starts "because your hand and your mind...".

### 4. Leonardo on diligence before speed
- English: "And remember to acquire diligence rather than rapidity."
- Italian: not in Richter's text; not checked.
- Author and work: Leonardo da Vinci, Notebooks, "What rules should be given to boys learning to paint", Richter section 491 (Vol. I, "Moral precepts for the student of painting").
- Edition: The Notebooks of Leonardo da Vinci, ed. and trans. Jean Paul Richter, 1883 (Gutenberg #5000).
- Seen at: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5000 (search "acquire diligence")
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Context that helps: the same section says to learn details "step by step" and not to move on "till you have the first well fixed in memory and in practice".

### 5. Leonardo on slow work building speed
- English: "And when you have thus schooled your hand and your judgment by such diligence, you will acquire rapidity before you are aware."
- Italian: not checked.
- Author and work: Leonardo da Vinci, Notebooks, "How that diligence [accuracy] should first be learnt rather than rapid execution", Richter section 492.
- Edition: Richter, 1883 (Gutenberg #5000).
- Seen at: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5000
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Note: pairs well with quote 4 on consecutive days. Good fit for slow, careful skill training.

### 6. Leonardo on obstacles
- English: "Obstacles cannot crush me. Every obstacle yields to stern resolve."
- Italian: not checked (Richter prints English only).
- Author and work: Leonardo da Vinci, Notebooks, Richter section 682 (a text written beside two sketches of a plough and compass).
- Edition: Richter, 1883 (Gutenberg #5000), Vol. I.
- Seen at: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5000
- Confidence: HIGH on wording. The next line in Richter is "He who is fixed to a star does not change his mind."
- Note: this is the line that circulates as "Obstacles cannot crush me" in a slightly shortened form. This version is the true Richter text.

### 7. Leonardo on keeping the mind in use
- English: "Just as iron rusts unless it is used, and water putrifies or, in cold, turns to ice, so our intellect spoils unless it is kept in use."
- Italian: not checked.
- Author and work: Leonardo da Vinci, Notebooks, Richter section 1177 (Vol. II, maxims).
- Edition: Richter, 1883 (Gutenberg #5000).
- Seen at: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5000 (search "iron rusts")
- Confidence: HIGH on wording. MEDIUM on the section number: the line sits after the footnote of 1177 and before 1178 in the Gutenberg text.
- Note: "putrifies" is Richter's spelling. For the card, cut to "...iron rusts unless it is used ... so our intellect spoils unless it is kept in use." and mark the ellipsis. It is about intellect, not hands, so use sparingly.

### 8. Alberti on study beating difficulty
- English: "Nothing is ever so difficult that study and application cannot conquer it."
- Italian: not checked.
- Author and work: Leon Battista Alberti, On Painting (Della pittura / De pictura, 1435-36), Book III.
- Edition: On Painting, trans. John R. Spencer, Yale University Press, rev. ed. 1966, around pp. 91-92 (page inferred from the noteaccess page markers).
- Seen at: https://www.noteaccess.com/Texts/Alberti/3.htm
- Confidence: HIGH on wording, MEDIUM on page.
- Alternative card from the same page: "Perfection in the art will be found with diligence, application and study." (Spencer, Book III, about p. 90.)
- Cross-check: Grayson's Penguin translation gives "the means of perfecting our art will be found in diligence, study, and application" (Penguin 1991, pp. 87-92 passim). Use only one translator per card.

### 9. Cellini on labouring at the craft
- English: "I devoted myself with sustained diligence and industry to acquiring mastery in the several branches of art..."
- Italian: not checked.
- Author and work: Benvenuto Cellini, Autobiography (Vita), Book I, chapter XXXI.
- Edition: The Life of Benvenuto Cellini, trans. John Addington Symonds (Gutenberg #4028; Symonds first published 1887).
- Seen at: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/4028 (search "sustained diligence")
- Confidence: HIGH on wording and chapter.
- Note: the trailing ellipsis is deliberate. The sentence continues "which I enumerated a short while back." For a clean card, end at "branches of art" and mark it.

### 10. Michelangelo on the hand that obeys the mind
- English (Symonds, rhymed): "The best of artists hath no thought to show / Which the rough stone in its superfluous shell / Doth not include: to break the marble spell / Is all the hand that serves the brain can do."
- Italian: "Non ha l'ottimo artista alcun concetto / c'un marmo solo in sé non circonscriva / col suo superchio, e solo a quello arriva / la man che ubbidisce all'intelletto."
- Author and work: Michelangelo Buonarroti, Rime, no. 151 (Guasti no. XV in Symonds' numbering), written about 1538-44.
- Edition: The Sonnets of Michael Angelo Buonarroti and Tommaso Campanella, trans. J. A. Symonds, 1878 (Gutenberg #10314), Sonnet XV.
- Seen at: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/10314/pg10314-images.html ; Italian at https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Rime_(Michelangelo)/151._Non_ha_l%27ottimo_artista_alcun_concetto
- Confidence: HIGH on authorship and wording. Over the 30-word limit, so show the last two lines only: "...to break the marble spell / Is all the hand that serves the brain can do."
- Caution: the poem is a love sonnet. The marble image is the opening of a conceit, not advice on practice. Keep the card framed as "skill of the hand".

## Reserve quotes (verified, use if one above is dropped)
- Leonardo, Richter section 19: "Practice must always be founded on sound theory." (Same section: those in love with practice without knowledge "are like the sailor who gets into a ship without rudder or compass".) Gutenberg #5000, Vol. I. HIGH.
- Leonardo, Richter section 498: "He is a poor disciple who does not excel his master." Gutenberg #5000. HIGH on English. Often quoted in Italian as "Tristo è quel discepolo che non avanza il suo maestro": not checked here.
- Vasari, Life of Niccola and Giovanni Pisano (De Vere trans., Macmillan and Medici Society, 1912, Vol. I, Gutenberg #25326): Niccola "put so much study and diligence into imitating this manner ... that he was judged, after no long time, the best sculptor of his day". English verified, page number not captured, so check before use. MEDIUM.
- Vasari, Life of Michelangelo (De Vere, Vol. IX, Gutenberg #32362): "up to the last day he had never ceased to work at something." This is Vasari's report, not Michelangelo's own words. English verified, page not captured. MEDIUM.

## Rejected as unverifiable or misattributed
- "Where the spirit does not work with the hand, there is no art" (Leonardo). Searched the full Richter text (Gutenberg #5000): no match. Quote-site claims of "Richter Vol. II section 1180" or "Treatise on Painting section 12" do not hold up. A fact-check page also finds it absent from Rigaud's Treatise and Wikiquote's sourced list, and the Italian looks like a back-translation: https://quotle.info/who-said/where-the-spirit-does-not-work-with-the-hand-there-is-no-art/ . Do not use.
- "Ancora imparo" / "I am still learning" (Michelangelo). Wikiquote traces it to Richard Duppa's Lives and Works of Michael Angelo and Raphael (1806), which wrongly credited a Domenico Giuntalodi drawing to Michelangelo; the motto was common in the 1500s. Spread by Disraeli (1823) and Emerson. No Michelangelo letter or poem carries it: https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Michelangelo . Do not use.
- "If people knew how hard I worked to gain my mastery, it would not seem so wonderful at all." No source in any known biography; first seen in popular books around 1929 (per Wikiquote notes mirrored at https://quotepark.com/authors/michelangelo-buonarroti). Do not use.
- "Genius is eternal patience", "Trifles make perfection, and perfection is no trifle", "I saw the angel in the marble and carved until I set him free": no letter, poem or early witness found: http://www.thehistoryofart.org/michelangelo/quotes . Do not use.
- "Study without desire spoils the memory" (Leonardo). The real Richter wording is different (sections 1175-1176): "study without a taste for it spoils memory, by retaining nothing which it has taken in." Use that if wanted. The popular "desire" version is a paraphrase.

## Notes for whoever builds the panel
- Store author, work, section, translator and edition with each quote, as above.
- All translations cited are public domain except Thompson 1933 (Yale/Dover) and Spencer 1966 (Yale). Short quotes are normally fine, but check if you quote more than a sentence in published marketing.
- Cennini and Alberti are early 15th century, Leonardo, Michelangelo and Cellini 16th century. All sit inside the 1350-1600 range.
