2. Depth perception

The gloves give the finger angles. They do not know where the hand is in the room. A phone with a depth sensor looks down at the table and finds each wrist. The engine joins the two.

Where the hand is. Sources: STATUS-lidar.md, STATUS-hand-root.md
Depth rate from the phone30 frames a second
Table surface fit error1.2 to 2 mm (root mean square)
Position jitter of the hand finder4.6 mm median, 27 mm at the 95th percentile

Jump gateA jump over 8 cm waits until 5 frames agree

3. Scoring

The score is an algorithm, not the AI Assistant. It uses dynamic time warping for each finger. Dynamic time warping compares two motions that run at different speeds.

How a repetition is judged. Sources: docs/CONTRACT.md, docs/LESSON_SYNC.md
ComparedEach of the five fingers against the expert's take, then the timing
CalibrationEach wearer records a flat hand and a fist. The scorer reads angles relative to those
Live number and final scoreBoth read the same calibrated angles
Finger weightsSet for each step. A table tennis grip weighs the thumb and index most
Same score on two machines19 score cases after sharing lessons: worst difference 0.000000

4. The AI Assistant, trained and served on Verda

We used Verda GPU containers for five jobs. A large open model, Gemma 4 31B, is the teacher. A small open model, Gemma 4 E2B, is the one we tuned. The tuning method is LoRA: a small set of added weights trained on top of a frozen model.

The five jobs. Source: docs/VERDA.md

A glove recording becomes a lesson (31B, one H200)0.656 of 6 valid lessons on the first try, 10 to 19 s each

Feedback model, then the load test (one H100)0.9994.8% tuned, 54.8% before tuning, 85.2% teacher

The tuned model. Sources: docs/COACH_MODEL.md, docs/VERDA.md
Training labels3,231 lines, written by the 31B teacher in 35 s
Added weights24.2 million, rank 16. The adapter file is 95 MB
Training3 epochs, 555 steps, 167 s on one H100
Held-out test1,150 examples from 9 units never seen in training
Real capture frames in that test333. Feedback 82.6%. Hints 62.2%, teacher 66.7%
Time for one hint0.108 s median, measured from Helsinki
Load on one H1000.10 to 0.14 s for each answer, up to 50 requests in flight
Learners for each GPUAbout 340 to 1,740 across runs, at the app's real rate of one hint in 5 s

What we say plainly:

  • The tuned model matches its 31B teacher on our held-out test. Training labels were filtered for correctness, so we do not say it is better.
  • Real glove recordings are test data only. On real frames the tuned hint model is below its teacher.
  • No Gemini model was distilled, and no Gemini output was used as training data.
  • The load runs varied and one run stalled. We give the range, and we do not know the cause of the stall.

5. The robot hand

A small policy performs expert takes on a simulated Unitree Dex3-1 hand. A policy is a network that picks the next joint targets.

The robot policy, simulation only. Source: docs/ROBOT_POLICY.md
Network3 hidden layers of 128, 45,703 weights
Error on held-out takes2.13 degrees
Plain replay of the take6.12 degrees
Replay sent early, the stronger baseline2.44 degrees
With pushes3.78 degrees against 7.25 for plain replay
Evaluation on the GPU1.44 million rollouts
Training time on a laptop44 s

The GPU did not improve the policy. It made the evaluation 40 times larger. There is no real hand and no contact in these numbers.
